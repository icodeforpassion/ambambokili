Publishing Checklist

YouTube Made for Kids Guide for Creators

If your videos are directed to children, publishing decisions matter as much as animation quality. This checklist focuses on practical steps creators should review before upload.

Set the audience accurately

Use YouTube's audience setting truthfully. Content directed to children should be identified appropriately. Correct designation also helps YouTube understand where the content belongs.

Keep titles and thumbnails representative

Do not use misleading reactions, unrelated characters or sensational text simply to attract clicks. Children's content should make it obvious what the viewer is about to watch.

Avoid keyword stuffing

Repeating “kids kids kids cartoon nursery rhyme baby” does not make a title more useful. Use a natural title that identifies the language, character, story or song.

Review commercial elements carefully

YouTube Kids has stricter rules around commercial and promotional content. If a video includes brands, endorsements or shopping-focused material, review current platform policies before publishing.

Check the story for unsafe imitation

Watch for scenes that could encourage risky copying, even when they are intended as jokes. Keep dangerous actions clearly outside the child's role and avoid presenting harmful behaviour as a challenge.

Use clear audio and age-appropriate language

Children's videos should not rely on overwhelming sound, frightening effects or confusing dialogue. For language-learning content, pronunciation and clarity are especially important.

Make metadata truthful

The description should explain what the video actually contains. Link to closely related videos and playlists rather than unrelated high-traffic topics.

Build a consistent channel identity

Recurring characters, visual style and themes help parents understand what the channel offers. Consistency is also more useful than chasing every trend.

Recheck policies periodically

Platform rules change. Treat this article as a production checklist, not legal advice, and check YouTube's current official guidance before major publishing or monetisation decisions.
